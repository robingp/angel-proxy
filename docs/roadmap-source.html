The build roadmap

What we build, and in what order

Each stage is useful on its own and can be released. We never build for a year before showing anyone.
NOW
Weeks 1–4

STEP 0 Check we are right — before writing code

Talk to twenty shop owners and freelancers. Run three small technical tests. No product code at all this month.

Why: one week of asking questions can save a year of building the wrong thing. This is the cheapest safety net we will ever have.
MONTHS
1 – 4

v0.1 The Checkout Monitor

The plugin quietly tests your shop over and over: can a customer see a product, add it to the basket, reach checkout, and see a way to pay? If any of that stops working, you know within minutes.

  • Only watches. It never changes anything on your site.
  • Tells you the exact step that broke and the exact file that caused it.
  • Stays quiet when everything is fine.
What you get: you stop finding out from an angry customer.
MONTHS
4 – 7

v0.2 The Change Timeline

The plugin remembers everything that changes on your site — every update, every setting, every new file, and who did it.

  • Still only watches. Nothing is changed for you yet.
  • Turns "it broke on Tuesday" into "these three things changed just before it broke."
What you get: you stop guessing what caused the problem.
MONTHS
7 – 12

v1.0 Safe Auto-Repair

Now the plugin is allowed to act. Before it changes anything it takes a copy so it can undo it. After it changes something it re-tests your shop. If the shop is broken, it puts everything back automatically — without you being there.

  • Nothing is ever deleted. Files are moved somewhere safe instead.
  • Anything risky asks you first, and shows you exactly what will change.
  • Every single action is written down in a report you can give a client.
What you get: updates stop being scary.
YEAR 2
and beyond

LATER Many sites, and AI supervision

Manage lots of sites from one place. Add checks for speed, search rankings and accessibility. And supervise the AI tools that are starting to make changes to WordPress sites on their own — making sure they cannot break anything either.

What happens next

The next 30 days

The most important month is the one with no coding in it

Before a single line is written, we find out whether shop owners actually feel this problem. Twenty conversations take about twenty hours. If nobody recognises the pain, we have lost two weeks instead of a year.

Weeks 1–2 · Ask, don't build

  • Talk to 10 shop owners and 10 freelancers or agencies.
  • Main question: "How do you know right now that your checkout works?"
  • Most will say: "a customer tells us." That answer is the whole business.
  • Also ask what the last breakage cost them.

Week 3 · Three small tests

  • Can the plugin test a checkout on cheap hosting?
  • Can it catch the exact error when a page crashes?
  • Can it run its checks on a schedule reliably?
  • Throwaway code. Two evenings each.

Week 4 · Cheap things, done in parallel

  • Check the name is legally free to use.
  • Decide the final name and buy the domain.
  • Put up one honest page and start collecting emails.

Then · Start building v0.1

  • Foundations first, then the checkout tests.
  • Give it free to 10–20 people quietly.
  • Fix what they struggle with before telling the world.

How we will know it is working

WhenGood signWarning sign
Week 2 People wince when asked about their last breakage. Nobody can remember one. Stop and rethink.
Week 3 All three technical tests work on cheap hosting. They fail — the plugin may only suit expensive hosting.
Month 4 Testers keep it installed and ask for more. They quietly uninstall it.
Month 6 Growing users, and support questions are dropping. Every spare hour goes on support. Stop growing, fix that first.
The one rule behind the whole plan: the plugin only watches at first, and is only allowed to change things once we have proved it is reliable. A watching plugin that has a bug shows the wrong status. A repairing plugin that has a bug can destroy a business.
