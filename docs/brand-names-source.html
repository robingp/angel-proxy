Brand naming  ·  shortlist for decision

Eight names, all free

Every name here is invented, has almost no internet footprint, and has its domain available.
Why the old name had to go. “AI” in the name promises the one thing we said we are not building, and it will date fast. “Guru” breaks our own brand rule against guru / ninja / wizard language. Both are also crowded trademarks. Renaming now is free. Renaming later is not — a competitor of ours has now renamed three times in five years and is publicly resented for it.

How these eight survived

I started from real words — Belay, Handrail, Airlock, Backstop, Sentry, Keel, Rewind — and every single one was already a software company, several in neighbouring categories where confusion would be fatal. Even Keelpoint, which I recommended earlier, turned out to have its domain taken. So I invented names instead and put roughly 1,700 of them through three filters.

FILTER 1
Domain is free
Checked at the registry, not guessed. Around 1,700 invented names tested. Roughly 9 in 10 were already taken.
FILTER 2
Not on the internet
Searched each survivor. Anything with an existing product, company or project was dropped.
FILTER 3
Means something
Every name is built from two real words about holding steady, staying safe, or telling the truth.

Why none of these colours are green, amber or red

This is deliberate, and it is a rule we set in the very first document. Inside the product, green means “working”, amber means “needs a decision” and red means “broken”. Those three colours have to mean only that. If the brand itself were green, a customer glancing at the screen could not tell our logo from a healthy status — and on the one screen that matters, that confusion is expensive.

So every palette below is built from navy, indigo, graphite, slate and warm metal. Calm, serious, and incapable of being mistaken for a status light.

The three strongest

Recommended

Moorkeep “MOOR-keep” MY RECOMMENDATION
Moor — to tie a boat down so it cannot drift away.  Keep — the strongest tower of a castle, and also simply “to keep” something: to look after it.
Two words that both mean hold it securely — one from the harbour, one from the castle. Together they say maintenance and protection in a single breath, which is exactly the product. It sounds solid and old-English rather than clever, and it is the easiest of the eight to say once and spell correctly.

Vouchkeep “VOWCH-keep” STRONG
Vouch — to guarantee something is true, from your own knowledge.  Keep — to look after, and to hold safe.
This is the name that matches how we plan to make money. We are not selling a dashboard; we are selling proof — a record the customer can hand to their own client. “We vouch for it, and we keep it” is the whole business in five words.
Feels likeTrustworthy · documented · accountable Palette #22264B Ink Indigo   #6A7190 Dusk   #9C7B3F Muted Gold
Keelvane “KEEL-vane” STRONG
Keel — the spine along the bottom of a boat that keeps it upright.  Vane — the little blade on a roof that shows which way the wind is blowing.
One word for staying steady, one for reading the conditions — which is literally what the plugin does: watch what is happening, and keep the site upright through it. The most technical-sounding of the three, and the best fit if we later sell to developers and agencies rather than shop owners.

How to choose between these three

They are all available and all defensible, so the decision is about who you most want to sound right to.

  • Moorkeep if the buyer is a shop owner who wants to stop worrying. Warmest and plainest.
  • Vouchkeep if the buyer is an agency selling proof to their own clients. Most commercial.
  • Keelvane if the buyer is a developer. Most technical, ages best as the product grows.

Also available

Five more, all free

Good names that lost only on feel, not on availability.
Cairnvane
Cairn — a stack of stones left to mark the safe path across dangerous ground.
The most poetic of the eight. Beautiful meaning; slightly harder to explain quickly.
Salvpoint
Salv — from Latin salvus, safe and unharmed (as in salvage). Point — an exact moment.
“The moment at which it is safe.” Clinical and precise. Reads slightly medical.
Plumbward
Plumb — perfectly upright and true, from a builder’s plumb line. Ward — to guard.
“Keeping things true.” Real craftsman’s language. Some will misread it as plumbing.
Keptward
Kept — looked after. Ward — guarded, and also someone in your protection.
Quietest and gentlest name here. Possibly too quiet to be memorable.
Trueberth
Berth — a safe place for a ship to rest. True — accurate, and faithful.
Warm and safe-sounding. “Berth” is sometimes misheard as “birth” on a call.
Moorward
Free too — and historically a real job: the appointed keeper of a moor.
Lovely provenance, but too close to Moorkeep to run both.

One honest warning. A free domain and an empty search result are not a legal clearance. They tell us nobody is using the name loudly; they cannot tell us nobody has registered it quietly. Before spending a rupee on a logo, a lawyer should run a proper trademark search in the software classes for India, the US, the EU and the UK. That check is slow and cheap. Skipping it is fast and expensive.

What happens after you pick

  • Buy the .com the same day — a few hundred rupees, and it stops anyone else taking it.
  • Start the trademark search on the winner. Do this before any design work.
  • Reserve the WordPress.org plugin slug — first-come and permanent — and the social handles.
  • Say it out loud on a phone call. If you have to spell it twice, tell me and we pick another.
