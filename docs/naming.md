# Product Naming

**Status:** shortlist ready for a decision. Legal clearance still required (§7).
**Resolves:** Phase 1 §12.3 — is the name open to revision?
**Method:** criteria from Phase 1 §11.3, candidates generated across seven directions, then collision-tested against live sources.

---

## 1. Why we are doing this

Phase 1 §11.3 raised three problems with **BuildWithGuru AI**, and nothing since has weakened them:

1. **"AI" contradicts the product.** The brief insists the AI is not the product; the name announces that it is. It also dates the brand — "AI" in a 2026 name will read the way "2.0" and "cloud" do now. Phase 2 §6.7 made this concrete: AI Engine is a capable plugin in a category with no defence, and WordPress core now ships its own AI client.
2. **"Guru" fights our own voice guide.** Phase 1 §4.2 bans guru/ninja/wizard/magic from our copy because they signal low-credibility marketing to an audience we are trying to persuade with engineering credibility. The name should not break the first rule of the brand.
3. **Trademark crowding.** "Guru" is heavily used in software and services classes.

Add a fourth, from Phase 2 §6.9: Solid Security has now been renamed **three times in five years** and is publicly resented for it. Renaming later is expensive and visible. Renaming now is free.

---

## 2. Criteria

| # | Criterion | Why |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Spellable and pronounceable on a support call | Real constraint for a support-light, solo-run product |
| C2 | No technology-of-the-moment terms | No "AI", no "smart", no "cloud" — they date |
| C3 | Distinctive enough to register | Descriptive names are weak marks and hard to defend |
| C4 | Suggests reliability or accountability | Not cleverness. Brand is calm, precise, plain-spoken (P1 §4.1) |
| C5 | Survives the product growing | From checkout monitoring → change governance → agent governance |
| C6 | Domain obtainable, WordPress.org slug free | Practical blockers |
| C7 | No "WordPress"/"Woo" in the name | Repository trademark rules (C1.1) |
| C8 | Reassuring to a shop owner, credible to an engineer | We sell to both Dana and Priya (P3 §3) |

**C8 is the one most naming exercises miss.** A name that thrills developers and unsettles a store owner fails. That rules out anything containing *fail*, *risk*, *break*, or *crash* — however apt they are to engineers.

## 3. What the research actually found

I collision-tested candidates against live sources. The headline result is more useful than any individual name:

> **Every plain-English safety metaphor worth having is already taken in software.**

| Candidate | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Belay** | ✗ Rejected | Multiple software companies, including a Swedish SaaS operating in Class 42 (quality management for audit firms), plus US companies trading under the name |
| **Handrail** | ✗ Rejected | A UX research SaaS (acquired by UserTesting) and a software-development firm both trade as Handrail |
| **Proofrail** | ✗ Rejected | **Taken — and see §4** |
| **Provenant** | ✗ Rejected | Verifiable-communications platform trading as Provenant |
| **Sentry / Sentinel** | ✗ Rejected | Sentry.io is error monitoring — adjacent category, fatal confusion risk |
| **Airlock** | ✗ Rejected | Airlock Digital (endpoint allowlisting) and Airlock (WAF vendor) — both security, both Class 42 |
| **Backstop** | ✗ Rejected | Backstop Solutions, institutional investment software |
| **Keel** (alone) | ✗ Rejected | An existing WordPress theme boilerplate on GitHub — same ecosystem, discovery confusion |
| **Rewind** | ✗ Rejected | Rewind.com is SaaS backup — directly adjacent |

**The lesson:** the pattern that wins in WordPress is not a dictionary word. It is a **distinctive coined compound** — Patchstack, Wordfence, Elementor, SeedProd. Those are registrable, ownable, and searchable. A common noun is none of those.

---

## 4. A competitive finding, not just a naming one

While testing "Proofrail" I found [**ProofRail**](https://www.proofrail.dev/) — *"governance for AI agent workflows"*, describing itself as a bypass-resistant control layer that mediates, monitors and constrains AI agent actions before they reach operational tools.

That is Phase 2 §3's position, built at the network layer instead of inside WordPress.

**It does not invalidate our position** — §7.2 of Phase 2 argued the governance layer must live where abilities are registered and invoked, which a network mediator cannot see, and nothing here is WordPress-specific. But it confirms the opportunity is real enough that others are moving on it, and it is worth watching. I have not assessed them properly; that belongs in a Phase 2 refresh, not here.

## 5. The shortlist

Three survivors, each strong in a different way. All still require legal clearance (§7).

### 5.1 **Keelpoint** — my recommendation

*The keel is the structural spine that keeps a vessel upright under load. "On an even keel" is exactly what we sell.*

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| C1 Spellable | ✓ Two common syllables, unambiguous |
| C2 Timeless | ✓ No trend words |
| C3 Registrable | ✓✓ **Strongest of the three.** A coined compound is arbitrary for software, which is the most defensible class of mark |
| C4 Reliability | ✓ Stability under motion — precisely the promise |
| C5 Survives growth | ✓ Nothing ties it to checkout, WooCommerce, or WordPress |
| C6 Availability | ✓ No software collisions found |
| C8 Both audiences | ✓ Calm and sturdy to a shop owner; structural and unshowy to an engineer |

**Weakness:** the metaphor needs one sentence of explanation. That is a fair price for a mark you can actually own, and the tagline does the work — *"Keelpoint. Your site, on an even keel."*

### 5.2 **Wellkept** — the warmest

*"Your site, well kept."* Says maintenance-with-care in one word, and is instantly warm to a non-technical owner.

**Strengths:** the best emotional fit of any candidate for Dana. Plain, calm, timeless, trivially spellable. No software collisions found.

**The problem, and it is a real one:** *well kept* is close to **descriptive** for a site-maintenance product. Descriptive marks are weak — hard to register, harder to defend, and a competitor can plausibly use the phrase generically. C3 is the criterion that exists to catch exactly this.

**Verdict:** lovely brand, risky asset. Viable if trademark counsel is comfortable, and a good tagline or product-line name regardless.

### 5.3 **Vouchsafe** — the most distinctive

*A real but uncommon word meaning "to grant or guarantee."* We vouch that the site is safe — and the word already contains both halves of the promise.

**Strengths:** highly distinctive and therefore highly registrable. Memorable. Directly expresses accountability, which is the Phase 2 §5.4 pricing position. Contains "safe" without being descriptive.

**Weaknesses:** faintly archaic; some people will need to hear it twice. **I have not collision-tested it** — it emerged after my search budget was better spent confirming rejections. It needs the same checks before it moves up.

## 6. Rejected directions, and why

- **Anything with WP or WordPress.** Fails C7 in the repository context and C5 in the long run. Elementor, Patchstack and Rank Math all thrive without it.
- **Anything with AI.** The original problem (§1).
- **Aviation words** (Preflight, Flightdeck) — apt, but *preflight* is a generic term in printing and prepress, making it descriptive and crowded.
- **Fear words** (Bulwark, Rampart, Bastion) — fail C8, and Phase 1 §4.2 forbids fear as a motivator. The security industry's fear marketing is exhausted and attracts the customer who buys in a panic and churns.
- **Failure words** (Failwell, Failsafe) — meaningful to engineers, alarming to shop owners. C8.
- **Obscure precision words** (Plimsoll, Kelson, Stanchion) — delightful, and they fail C1 on the first support call.

---

## 7. What still has to happen — in this order

**None of the above is clearance.** A web search finds companies that market loudly; it does not find registered marks that don't. These are the actual steps:

1. **Professional trademark search** on the chosen name in the relevant classes — Class 9 (software) and Class 42 (SaaS), in the territories that matter (at minimum India, US, EU, UK). This is Phase 2 §13.4 and it is the one item that is cheap now and expensive to skip.
2. **Domain check** — `.com` first, then a credible alternative. Do not commit to a name before the domain is secured.
3. **WordPress.org slug availability** — the plugin slug is first-come and permanent. Check before announcing anything.
4. **Social handles** — cheap, and awkward to fix later.
5. **Say it out loud on a phone call.** If it has to be spelled twice, it fails C1 regardless of what the search says.

**Order matters:** search first, then buy. Buying a domain for a name that fails clearance is how naming budgets get wasted.

---

## 8. Recommendation

> **Keelpoint**, subject to clearance. **Vouchsafe** as the fallback if clearance blocks it, once tested. **Wellkept** only if counsel is relaxed about descriptiveness.

The reasoning in one line: *Keelpoint is the only candidate that is simultaneously registrable, calm, credible to both buyers, and unconstrained by what the product becomes in five years.*

And the decision that matters more than which name wins: **start the clearance search now.** It is slow, it is cheap, and every week without it is a week of building brand equity that might have to be thrown away — which is precisely what happened to Solid Security, three times.
